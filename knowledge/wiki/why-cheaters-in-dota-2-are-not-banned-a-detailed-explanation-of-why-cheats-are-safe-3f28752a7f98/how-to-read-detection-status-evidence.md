## How to read detection-status evidence

A status page can responsibly say that a specific build was tested on a specific date and that no issue was observed in that test. It cannot turn that limited observation into “safe forever.” Ask how the status is updated, whether unavailable periods remain visible and whether the seller distinguishes technical failure from confirmed enforcement.

User reports also need context. The product version, other installed tools, date of use and date of enforcement may all be unknown. One report cannot prove a universal cause, while silence cannot prove absence. Treat aggregated reports as warning signals, not a scientific detection rate.

The honest conclusion is conditional: a feature may be working now, but the account remains exposed to software, platform and future-update risk. That wording is less dramatic and more accurate.

Separate product uptime from detection status as well. A server can be online while a module is broken; a module can function while enforcement is delayed. A single green badge should not represent delivery, technical compatibility and account safety at the same time. Each claim needs its own date and meaning.

When several products are installed, attribution becomes even weaker. A user cannot reliably identify which component created a signal, so stacking tools makes public status reports less useful and increases the number of files that must be trusted.
