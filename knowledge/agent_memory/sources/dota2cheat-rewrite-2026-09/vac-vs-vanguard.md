**VAC and Riot Vanguard are different anti-cheat products, but neither difference proves that a third-party cheat is safe.** VAC is Valve's anti-cheat system used across Steam games; Vanguard is Riot's anti-cheat platform for VALORANT. Their public documentation describes different components and support flows, while detection coverage and enforcement logic remain incomplete from an outside reader's view.

The comparison below sticks to what Valve, Steam, and Riot publish. It does not rank products or explain how to bypass either system.

![Image 2: VAC and Riot Vanguard anti-cheat comparison](https://dota2cheat.com/assets/dota2/dota2-f734e4d8e81d669199c6d012.webp)

## Comparison at a glance

| Criterion | VAC / Steam | Riot Vanguard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Publisher and game context | Valve's anti-cheat policy applies to VAC-enabled Steam games, including Dota 2. | Riot's anti-cheat platform supports VALORANT and its account enforcement. |
| Public policy | Steam explains that third-party modifications giving an advantage can trigger a VAC ban and that VAC bans are permanent. | Riot Support explains Vanguard's role and publishes separate restrictions, error, and uninstall guidance. |
| Architecture detail | Valve publishes selected examples, not a complete detection map. | Riot documents client and system requirements, but not a complete detection signature list. |
| Enforcement visibility | Visible VAC, game, or matchmaking actions are not interchangeable labels. | A Vanguard restriction or VALORANT penalty is governed by Riot's account and support process. |
| Safe-cheat conclusion | None. A clean launch does not cancel the policy. | None. A product claim cannot override Riot's rules. |

## What VAC documentation actually establishes

Steam's [VAC guidance](https://help.steampowered.com/en/faqs/view/571A-97DA-70E9-FF74) says a third-party modification that gives an advantage is a cheat, and that VAC bans are permanent and not manually removed by Steam Support. Valve's Dota 2 enforcement report also describes a deliberately hidden data area used to identify software reading information ordinary clients should not read.

![Image 3: Valve anti-cheat client data detection example](https://dota2cheat.com/assets/dota2/dota2-d2699db607b62b946d3bcb34.webp)

Those sources establish a policy boundary and one detection example. They do not reveal every signal, the time between detection and enforcement, or the status of a particular private tool.

## What Riot publishes about Vanguard

Riot's official Vanguard support documentation describes Vanguard as VALORANT's anti-cheat system and links to restrictions, error-code, and uninstall guidance. Riot's support pages are the source for current requirements and troubleshooting. A forum post or seller badge is not a substitute for those pages.

![Image 4: Anti-cheat client interface example](https://dota2cheat.com/assets/dota2/dota2-ea6e76fab8f8bcc30e249613.webp)

Deployment differences can affect startup, drivers, compatibility, and privacy questions. They do not turn an unauthorised game modification into an approved one. If a tool asks for broad system access, evaluate the security and account consequences separately from the anti-cheat brand.

## Keep the categories separate

*   **Architecture:** what the vendor publicly says is installed or checked.
*   **Policy:** what the game rules classify as an advantage.
*   **Enforcement:** what account message or restriction a user sees.
*   **Evidence:** whether a claim has a dated, primary source.

Do not use one category to infer another. A kernel component does not prove complete coverage; a user who is not yet banned does not prove a tool is invisible; and one public ban does not reveal the entire detection system.

![Image 5: Anti-cheat system maintenance and update example](https://dota2cheat.com/assets/dota2/dota2-4089b23118af2faf2e7d8e0b.webp)

## Privacy and maintenance are separate questions

Anti-cheat architecture is only one part of the decision. Read the vendor's current documentation for startup behavior, update requirements, driver or service removal, supported operating systems, and what happens when the component is disabled. A broad system permission can be a security and compatibility concern even when no game penalty occurs. Conversely, a smaller client component is not proof that coverage is weak.

Record the source and date for each claim. Riot and Valve can change services, clients, and enforcement workflows after this article is published. Revisit the official Vanguard support page and Valve's VAC guidance instead of treating an old comparison as a current specification.

Do not compare products by a single word such as “kernel” or “cloud.” Ask what the component does at startup, which game it protects, how updates are delivered, and which support page explains removal. A precise question produces a useful answer; a broad label produces another marketing argument.

## How to evaluate a claim

1.   Open the current Valve or Riot support page.
2.   Identify the game, account, visible message, and date.
3.   Separate a product's feature claim from its detection claim.
4.   Check update and uninstall requirements before installing any component.
5.   Reject “undetected forever,” guaranteed unban, and bypass language.
