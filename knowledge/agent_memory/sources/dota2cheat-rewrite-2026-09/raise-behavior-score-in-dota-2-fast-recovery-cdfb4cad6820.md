**Raise Dota 2 behavior score** by removing the behaviors that create negative conduct signals, not by chasing a secret queue, paid account swap or one-week promise. Valve does not publish a formula that converts a specific number of matches or commends into a guaranteed score increase.

A recovery plan should therefore focus on actions you control: completing matches, preventing disconnects, limiting hostile communication and using one secure account consistently.

## Start with the cause you can verify

Review recent conduct summaries and your own match history. Separate technical abandons from communication problems, intentional griefing and reports caused by repeated conflict. One plan cannot fix all four.

If connection failures are the main problem, stop queuing until the network, power and game installation are stable. Finishing a match calmly is more useful than starting several sessions that may become abandons.

## Use a low-conflict communication rule

Keep messages short, factual and related to the next action. Do not debate blame during the match. Mute escalating conversations early rather than answering every provocation. Pings should communicate information, not punish a teammate.

A simple rule works: one useful call, no personal criticism, then silence if the exchange becomes hostile. This protects focus and reduces the number of interactions that can generate reports.

## Choose roles and heroes you can finish with

Recovery is not the time to force an unfamiliar role in every match. Use a small hero pool, queue when you can complete the full game and avoid experimenting with strategies that require reluctant teammates to cooperate.

Winning is helpful, but behavior score is not simply win rate. A calm loss with normal participation is different from an abandon or a match spent arguing.

## Do not use account rotation as recovery

A new or purchased account does not repair the conduct history of the main account and may create a separate smurfing problem. Valve has publicly linked smurf accounts to main accounts in enforcement.

Read [Valve's official smurf-account enforcement notice](https://www.dota2.com/newsentry/3692442542242977036) and our [Dota 2 smurf-ban and account recovery guide](https://cheatsgaming.com/games/dota-2/how-to-avoid-a-smurf-ban-in-dota-2-guide-to-choosing-an-account-for-dota-f2e7434083ca) before accepting account-market shortcuts.

## A four-week conduct routine

1.   **Before queue:** verify connection, time and mental state.
2.   **During draft:** state role and one plan without confrontation.
3.   **During play:** use concise calls and mute conflict.
4.   **After play:** take a break after two emotionally difficult matches.
5.   **Each week:** review abandons and repeated triggers, not only score movement.

No one can guarantee the rate of recovery. Consistency is the measurable goal. The [Dota 2 behavior, bans and account guide hub](https://cheatsgaming.com/games/dota-2) contains the related policy articles.

## Measure inputs instead of chasing a daily number

Track the factors you can verify each week: completed matches, abandons, muted conflicts, reports visible in summaries and sessions ended early because you were tilted. Behavior score may update in batches, so checking after every match creates noise and encourages superstition.

Do not purchase commends, queue manipulation or account boosting. Those services cannot guarantee the quality of the accounts involved and may introduce coordinated abuse or credential theft. A temporary number change is not a stable recovery if the underlying behavior remains.

When improvement stalls, reduce volume before changing strategy. Fewer well-timed matches with reliable completion usually provide a cleaner environment for changing habits than an all-day grind under frustration.

Commends can reflect positive play, but asking strangers to trade them turns the metric into a target instead of feedback. Focus on useful calls, role participation and finishing the match. If those inputs improve, genuine positive signals can follow without a coordinated exchange.

Use the same review window every week. Changing heroes, queue times and communication style every day makes it harder to identify which habit reduced conflict or prevented abandons.

Keep notes private and factual rather than naming teammates. The purpose is to change your own repeatable inputs, not build a record of blame.
