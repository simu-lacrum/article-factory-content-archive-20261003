## Cheats and scripts — terminology

In Dota 2, these two words often appear together, so let’s take a look at how they differ.

Among users, **scripts are commonly used to refer to individual functions** that simplify gameplay, automatically use key combinations or mouse movements, or replace something.

In turn, **a cheat is a program that contains all these scripts**, ensures their safe operation, and provides an interface for interacting with the functions. In other words, it is a kind of complex of programs and functions.

So when you hear a word like Melonity, it refers specifically to the name of [the dota cheat and the scripts](https://melonity.gg/?r=medium) it contains.

As you understand, no one can ever give a 100% guarantee that your account will not be blocked in the game. Most people play with cheats in Dota because it opens up the game in a new way, giving people the opportunity to experience emotions from the game that they haven’t felt in a long time.

However, there are currently ways to protect against VAC in cheats that guarantee almost 100% protection against it. I suggest we take a brief look at how VAC works so that you can gain a deeper understanding of what we are talking about.

### How does VAC work?

There are two main types of anti-cheats in games: server-side and client-side, the latter having several levels of access.

VAC has a client module that is responsible for checking the integrity of game files, as well as a server module that checks what orders you send to the server with mouse clicks, etc. There are a whole bunch of such checks, which cannot be listed here.

There are also kernel anti-cheats that run directly in the BIOS, such as Vanguard or Faceit. These are often cited as the best anti-cheats, but their design is also their biggest drawback.

Unlike VAC, which only monitors the game itself, these anti-cheats have full access to the files on your computer. This means that Vanguard, for example, could potentially access your data if it wanted to. There are no documented cases of this happening, but there are warning signs. Moreover, similar anti-cheats have learned to bypass the hypervisor, and teams from Riot and Faceit are currently working on this issue.
