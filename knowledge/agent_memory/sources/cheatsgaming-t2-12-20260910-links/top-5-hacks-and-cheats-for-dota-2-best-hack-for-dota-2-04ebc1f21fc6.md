## Start with live status, not the feature count

A product should show when its status was checked and whether the current Dota 2 build is supported. “Available” without a date is weak evidence. If a patch has just landed, a temporary updating state is more credible than an instant promise that everything works.

Next, confirm operating system, architecture and platform. A Windows 11 listing is not automatically compatible with every Windows build, virtual machine or security configuration. Requirements should be explicit before checkout.

## Read the access plan as a contract

A useful plan states the duration, currency, final price, activation method and when the clock starts. It should also explain what happens while the product is unavailable. Do not assume downtime is compensated unless the terms say so.

Automatic key delivery reduces waiting, but it does not replace support. Confirm where activation happens, whether one key is tied to one account or device, and which contact channel handles failed activation.

## Reject impossible safety promises

Valve does not publish a complete detection checklist. In [Valve's documented Dota 2 ban-wave report](https://www.dota2.com/newsentry/3677788723152833273), the company explained that a hidden client-data signal was used to identify a large group of accounts. A seller cannot prove that future detection is impossible.

*   Avoid “zero ban chance” and lifetime-undetected claims.
*   Expect status changes after major patches.
*   Keep valuable accounts separate from experiments you may later regret.
*   Read refund and downtime terms before paying.

4.   Are delivery and activation explained before payment?

## Red flags that end the evaluation

Stop when a seller asks for Steam credentials, hides the final price until direct messages, cannot identify the supported build or treats all downtime questions as confidential. The same applies to copied testimonials with no date, screenshots from another product and support channels that cannot be verified from the listing.

Quality is also visible in restraint. A credible page can say that a module is temporarily unavailable, that a hero is unsupported or that no safety guarantee exists. Those limitations help a buyer make a decision; pretending that every feature always works does not.

Make the final choice from the listing itself, not a reseller's chat summary. Save the plan terms and status date you relied on. If the price, duration or delivery method changes between the product page and invoice, stop before payment and ask for a corrected order.
