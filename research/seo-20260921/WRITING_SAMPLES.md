# Voice samples for editorial review

These are example openings, not finished articles or claims of product testing. They illustrate the target voice: plain English, specific distinctions, no padded introductions.

## CS2 triggerbot vs aimbot

A triggerbot and an aimbot automate different parts of a shot. A triggerbot concerns when the shot fires. An aimbot concerns where the aim goes. A product can advertise both, which is why a feature list full of the word “aim” tells you surprisingly little.

Picture a target crossing a crosshair that is already in place. Firing at that moment is one action. Moving the crosshair onto the target is another. That distinction is the useful starting point when you read a CS2 feature description.

Next, look for scope. Does the page describe only firing assistance, or does it also claim target selection and aim movement? If the wording never gets that specific, the honest answer is that the feature list is incomplete.

Editorial basis: the [Cheatix feature overview](https://cheatix.to/information/counter-strike-2/best-cheats) separates these labels. The hypothetical example explains the distinction; it is not a test result.

## Deadlock souls aimbot

“Aimbot” is a broad label in Deadlock. Before comparing two feature lists, ask what each one is supposed to target: a player, a projectile-related aim point, or a soul orb. Those are different jobs.

If you came here looking for a souls feature, a paragraph about aiming at enemy heroes has not answered your question. You need a clear description of the target type, the action the feature claims to assist, and anything the description leaves uncertain.

That is also why a longer feature list does not automatically make a better comparison. “Aimbot” repeated under three headings can be three different features—or three names for overlapping behavior. The wording needs to make that distinction visible.

Editorial basis: [Avalanche's feature page](https://avalan.cc/features) and [Tsuki's feature page](https://tsuki.gg/products/deadlock/features) are retained as vendor statements. Check the saved passages before turning any specific capability into a product claim.

## Dota 2 scripts, macros and bots

“Dota 2 script” can send you to three very different places: a hero-automation product, a repeated input macro, or a bot script made for a supported development environment. Start by naming which one you mean. Otherwise, you can spend five minutes reading a perfectly detailed answer to the wrong question.

For a hero feature, the useful question is which action the software claims to automate. For a macro, it is which inputs are repeated. For a bot script, it is the environment the script was written for. Sharing the word “script” does not make them interchangeable.

That gives the article a clear job: sort the terms first, then link the reader to the right explanation. A product mention belongs after that distinction, where it can illustrate one category without pretending to define the whole subject.

Editorial basis: the captured Google “dota 2 scripts” sample mixes macro pages, repositories and product pages. This opening resolves that observed ambiguity without inventing personal experience.
