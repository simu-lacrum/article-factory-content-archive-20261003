## Evidence hierarchy

Use evidence in this order:

1. Current official product page, status page, documentation or dated changelog.
2. Current official support announcement.
3. Independent review that discloses its testing method and date.
4. Community reports only to surface questions or failure patterns, never to prove safety, detection or product quality.

For every comparison claim, record:

- URL;
- retrieval date;
- whether it is directly observed or vendor-stated;
- the exact criterion it supports;
- what remains unknown.

## Shared comparison criteria

Do not build a giant checkbox matrix. Compare only criteria that change a buyer’s decision:

- current availability and last visible update;
- game coverage and product focus;
- public documentation and changelog quality;
- feature categories relevant to the article’s scenario;
- operating-system support;
- trial and subscription model;
- support route and published hours, if any;
- configuration portability and account model;
- clarity of limitations and risk disclosure.

Do not compare “detection rate” unless all providers publish equivalent, independently verifiable data. They do not.

## Required unique-value layer

Every final article must add one original editorial artifact:

- a dated claim ledger;
- a decision tree;
- a seven-day evaluation worksheet;
- a status-verification checklist;
- a scenario card;
- or a public-evidence scorecard with explained weights.

This prevents thin affiliation and gives the reader something beyond rewritten product copy.

## E-E-A-T and naturalness rules

- Name the author or reviewer and state the review date.
- Disclose whether products were actually tested. If they were not, say that the comparison is based on public product evidence.
- Never invent first-person testing, ban history, screenshots or support conversations.
- Use concrete observations: “the page lists”, “the changelog dated…”, “the status page reports”.
- Vary paragraph and sentence length; avoid identical section counts and repeated transition formulas.
- Include a real limitation that can change the verdict.
- Let the conclusion choose by use case; do not crown a universal winner.
- Search the final H1 and two distinctive sentences before publication.

Google’s people-first and review guidance should be applied as the quality bar: original analysis, a visible method, meaningful value beyond source summaries, and clear evidence of who created or reviewed the page.

## Safety boundary

Allowed: product positioning, high-level feature categories, public documentation, pricing structure, compatibility, support and risk-aware decision criteria.

Not allowed: installation walkthroughs, bypass instructions, injection methods, detection-evasion settings, “humanization” recipes, memory/driver details, disabling antivirus or instructions intended to conceal cheating.
