## Link rules for every article

- One main backlink to the exact target in the brief. Do not add a second exact-match commercial link.
- Put it naturally at 35–65% of the body, after the article has already delivered a useful explanation.
- Do not place it in the first paragraph, FAQ, conclusion, caption or image.
- The recommended anchor is contextual, not a naked URL and not a promise of safety.
- Treat the destination as a next reading step, shortlist or reference point. A ranking is not proof of current status, compatibility or zero risk.
- For Dota 2 HOLD briefs, omit the backlink entirely until the 404 condition is fixed.

## Writing protocol: natural, original and useful

“100% unique” can only be measured against a named corpus with a named checker. The operational requirement here is stricter in a useful way: write every article from a blank page, use its own thesis and scene, do not paraphrase the destination, and run the client's chosen similarity check before publication.

Every article must contain:

- one concrete opening situation, not a generic definition;
- one limitation or counterexample where the obvious advice fails;
- one practical inspection method, checklist or decision sequence;
- one clearly marked boundary between verified fact, source claim and author inference;
- varied paragraph length and sentence rhythm;
- a short conclusion with one next action, not a recap of every H2;
- an FAQ block at the end.

Delete or rewrite these AI-like habits during line edit:

- stock openings such as “in today's fast-paced world”, “let's dive in”, “whether you are a beginner or a veteran”;
- repeated contrast frames such as “not X, but Y” in neighboring paragraphs;
- mechanically symmetrical paragraphs and identical three-item lists;
- a mini-summary after every section;
- unsupported confidence, invented testing and fake first-person experience;
- decorative em dashes, excessive semicolons, title-case subheads and bolding every other phrase;
- generic “this powerful tool offers a wide range of features” prose;
- conclusions that only restate the introduction.

The goal is editorial quality, not detector gaming. Automated AI detectors produce false positives; human fact-check and line edit remain mandatory.

## Safety and fact-check protocol

- No instructions for injection, bypass, evasion, drivers, process hiding, offsets, signatures or memory access.
- Never tell readers to disable antivirus, Defender, Secure Boot or other safeguards.
- No `100% safe`, `undetected`, `no bans`, `never detected`, `zero risk` or equivalent wording.
- Prices, trials, subscription terms, current support, compatibility, patch status and detection claims must be rechecked on the publication date.
- For installation destinations, write source-verification, troubleshooting and support-reporting content only. Do not reproduce the destination's operational steps.
- For ban/safety destinations, explain uncertainty, evidence quality and time-bounded claims. Do not provide evasion advice.
- Use official Valve/Steam/game sources for game and enforcement facts whenever available. Community anecdotes may reveal questions, not prove mechanisms.

## SEO acceptance

- Primary keyword appears verbatim in H1, description and naturally within the first 120 words.
- Description stays at or below 160 Unicode characters.
- One H1 only; H2s follow the search task, not a keyword template.
- No markdown tables in the article.
- No year in the slug unless the topic truly expires annually.
- Search the final H1 and two distinctive sentences before publication; if a close match appears, change the reasoning angle, not just synonyms.
