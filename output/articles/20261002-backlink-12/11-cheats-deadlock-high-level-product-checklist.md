# Cheats Deadlock: A High-Level Product Checklist

The search phrase **cheats deadlock** is broad enough to hide several different reader jobs. Some people want a hero feature glossary, others want visual information, and others are checking whether a product page is current. Start by naming the job. Then verify scope, dates and support instead of trusting a universal claim.

## A page is a claim map

Read a Deadlock catalogue as four layers:

- **Hero scope:** which hero or mechanic is named?
- **Feature scope:** is the term a visual feature, aim category, camera label or automation claim?
- **Update evidence:** is there a date, changelog or known-issue note?
- **Support:** can the reader ask a specific compatibility question?

The page may be commercially useful while still leaving some answers unknown. That is a normal result, not a reason to invent a verdict.

## Why hero coverage needs context

A product can list “hero combos” or “aim” without explaining which characters, modes or updates are included. One demo clip shows one context. It does not establish universal coverage or an account outcome.

Record the smallest claim the evidence supports:

1. Feature name visible.
2. Hero and mode named.
3. Source date recorded.
4. Missing boundaries marked.

## Product link without a fake endorsement

## What to avoid in a review

Do not turn “updated” into “works forever,” or “private” into “safe.” Do not include bypass, stealth, injection or evasion steps. A high-level product checklist is enough to help a reader decide what needs verification next.
