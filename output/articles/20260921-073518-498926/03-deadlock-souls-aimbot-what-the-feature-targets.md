# Deadlock Souls Aimbot: What the Feature Targets

A **Deadlock souls aimbot** is described as aim assistance for souls rather than enemy heroes. That target distinction matters: a feature list headed “Aimbot” may not tell you which of those jobs it covers.

[Cluster's Deadlock page](https://cluster.center/en/deadlock), for example, lists Aimbot and Souls Aimbot separately. Its descriptions distinguish enemy-hero targeting from soul targeting. This is the vendor's stated scope, not an independent performance verdict.

## Start with the target, then the action

Two questions clear up much of the confusion:

1. **What is being targeted?** A player and a soul are not interchangeable targets in a feature description.
2. **What action changes?** Moving aim, firing and displaying information are separate functions.

A soul highlighted on screen, a crosshair moving toward it and a shot being fired are three different events. A feature can claim one, two or all three. Write down the actions before comparing the names.

For a player-target feature, start a separate line. An explanation about tracking enemy heroes has not yet answered your question about souls.

## Soul aimbot, soul triggerbot and soul ESP

These names can sit next to each other while describing different actions:

- **Soul aimbot:** a claim about aiming at souls.
- **Soul triggerbot:** a claim about firing associated with souls.
- **Soul ESP:** a claim about displaying or highlighting soul information.

Actual wording still matters. [Avalanche's feature page](https://avalan.cc/features) describes its soul triggerbot in terms of firing when an orb is on the crosshair and lists soul highlighting separately. [Tsuki's feature page](https://tsuki.gg/products/deadlock/features) uses a soul-triggerbot description that includes aiming movement as well as firing.

Put those two descriptions side by side and a yes/no checklist breaks down: the same label covers different advertised actions. Keeping the verb beside the name makes the difference visible.

## Check what “FOV” belongs to

Another easy mix-up is treating every FOV label as a camera setting. [Tsuki's Aimed FOV page](https://tsuki.gg/scripts/deadlock/aimed-fov) describes a custom FOV for its aimbot while aiming. That wording concerns the aiming feature; it does not describe changing the camera view.

Keep the context in your notes: camera, player aim or soul aim. A bare “FOV supported” entry loses that distinction. The short description also leaves implementation details open, so it cannot establish how another product handles the same label.

## What a short demo cannot settle

Suppose a recording shows an orb being hit. That can illustrate an event in the recording. It doesn't, on its own, tell you which feature was responsible, what other functions were active or how representative that moment is.

For a claim about consistent performance, look for the conditions and failures as well as the successful moment. Otherwise, you have a feature description and a clip—not a measured success rate.

## FAQ

### Is a Deadlock souls aimbot the same as a player aimbot?

The names refer to different target types. Cluster's public page lists those categories separately; another product's scope needs its own description checked.

### Is soul ESP an aiming feature?

ESP describes an information display. Do not infer aiming or firing from an ESP label alone.

### Does “soul triggerbot” always mean exactly the same thing?

No. The sampled vendor descriptions differ in whether they describe firing alone or also mention aiming movement. Compare the actions in the description.

### Does a longer feature list prove better performance?

No. It may list more categories, overlapping labels or more detailed descriptions. Performance needs evidence beyond the number of items on the page.
