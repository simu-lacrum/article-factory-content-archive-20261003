# Dota 2 Cheat Detection: VAC Facts and Limits

Dota 2 cheat detection cannot be reduced to a public “detected list.” Valve can change client signals, server checks, review processes and enforcement timing. A tool that launches without a visible penalty has only avoided a visible penalty during that window.

> **Quick answer:** read VAC policy as a boundary, treat delayed reports as weak evidence and mark every claim about current detection status as time-sensitive unless an official source confirms it.

## What Steam’s VAC policy says

Steam Support describes VAC as Valve Anti-Cheat and explains the general consequences of cheating in VAC-secured games. That is useful policy evidence. It is not a full public map of Dota 2’s internal checks, every signal or every enforcement decision.

Do not turn a policy page into a product review. It tells you what Valve prohibits and how bans are described, not whether a specific third-party tool is currently detected.

## Why delayed reports are weak evidence

Community posts often compare a launch date with a later ban and call the gap proof of safety. It is not. The post may omit account history, the tool version, the match context or the enforcement cause. A delayed action can have many explanations, and a missing action proves even less.

Use careful labels:

- official policy;
- vendor statement;
- player report;
- independent test with a documented method;
- unknown.

Those categories should never be merged into one confident sentence.

## Separate the layers of risk

There are at least three layers:

1. **Account policy:** whether a behavior violates the platform’s rules.
2. **Technical security:** what a download or executable can access.
3. **Feature evidence:** whether the claimed module works on the current build.

A clean file scan does not answer the policy question. A working clip does not answer the account question. A quiet account does not answer either one permanently.

## How to read a product status page

Look for a dated build note, supported game version, changelog, support route and clear unknowns. Be cautious with permanent “undetected” labels, countdowns and screenshots that show no date.

After a Dota update, re-check the listing rather than copying yesterday’s status into a new article. Freshness is part of the claim.

## What public sources cannot answer

Public pages cannot establish every internal signal, exact enforcement timing or a zero-ban outcome. Do not offer readers evasion steps or operational detection-avoidance advice. The responsible conclusion is that third-party cheat use carries unresolved account risk.

Melonity is the mapped Dota 2 product for CheatsGaming. Its status page and feature claims must be read as vendor material, not as proof that VAC will or will not act. This mention is commercial and makes no safety promise.

## FAQ

### Is Dota 2 cheat detection public?

Only partly. Valve publishes policy and support information, not a complete list of internal signals or enforcement timing.

### Does “not banned yet” prove a cheat is safe?

No. It is a limited observation with unknown future enforcement and unknown account history.

### Can a product page guarantee no ban?

No credible guarantee exists. Treat permanent safety labels as marketing claims.

### What should I check after a Dota patch?

Re-check official updates, the product’s dated status and any support notes. Do not assume an old clip remains current.

### How should I describe player reports?

Label them as player reports, preserve the date and method and do not present them as official proof.
