## What you can configure in cluster.center

cluster.center is not just one button. The local CS2 memory describes several feature groups, and they matter because most players search for a CS2 hack without knowing which part they actually need.

### Aimbot

The Aimbot side is about aim assistance. The useful settings to understand are FOV, Smooth, Auto Pistol, target priority, hitboxes, checks, and weapon profiles.

In plain language:

- FOV controls the aim-assist area around the crosshair.
- Smooth controls how gradual the movement feels.
- Target priority decides which target is preferred.
- Hitboxes include areas like head, neck, spine, hips, arms, and legs.
- Checks can include visible, team, and flash logic.
- Weapon profiles let rifles, pistols, and sniper rifles feel different.

If you are new to a CS2 cheat, start soft. Obvious aim behavior is usually the fastest way to make the experience feel bad.

### TriggerBot

TriggerBot is about shot timing. In cluster.center memory, the important settings include reaction time, delay between shots, minimum damage, hit chance, hitboxes, and visibility/team/flash checks.

This is the kind of feature players usually use for holding angles. Keep the idea simple: it should feel controlled, not random.

### ESP

ESP is the visual-awareness part of a hack for CS2. The local feature list includes Box, Name, HealthBar, AmmoBar, Weapon, Chams, Skeleton, Bomb, and Defuse indicators.

Good ESP is readable. Bad ESP turns the screen into noise.

Start with only what you can actually use:

- Box or Skeleton for player position;
- HealthBar for quick fights;
- Weapon if you care about peeking decisions;
- Bomb and Defuse information for round decisions.

### Hub

The Hub utilities are small, but useful.

cluster.center memory lists:

- Bomb timer for the time left before explosion;
- Spectator List for observer awareness;
- Keybinds for tracking active hotkeys.

These are not flashy features, but they make a CS2 hack easier to control during real rounds.
