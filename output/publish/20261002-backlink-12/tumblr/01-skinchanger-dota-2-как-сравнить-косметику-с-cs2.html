The four-question comparison

  1. What is in the catalogue? Record the named category and one example item.
  2. Where is it rendered? A local client frame is not automatically a server-visible state.
  3. What can other players see? Require a stated viewing context; otherwise mark the claim unknown.
  4. What does the account own? Inventory, trade, and market status need account-level evidence.

This avoids a common mistake: treating a polished preview as proof of a new item in Steam or a permanent loadout.

A better way to read a product screenshot

Look for the smallest claim the image supports. A menu can support “the menu contains a cosmetic selector.” A match frame can support “this frame displays a changed model.” Neither, by itself, proves current compatibility, visibility to other players, transferability, or safety.

Make a note for each image:

  • source and date;
  • game and mode;
  • object shown;
  • what remains unverified.

That four-line record is more useful than a “best skinchanger” badge with no boundaries.
